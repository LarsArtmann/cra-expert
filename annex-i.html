Annex I, Part I — properties of products with digital elements

The chapeau: products must ensure an appropriate level of cybersecurity based on risk (Part I, point 1). Point 2 then lists the properties below, on the basis of the Art 13(2) risk assessment.

No known exploitable vulnerabilities on market placement.

Evidence: pre-release security testing records; scanner output triaged to an SLA (e.g. Trivy/Syft + OSV); VEX statements suppressing investigated non-issues; a release gate policy.

Secure by default configuration (unless agreed otherwise for tailor-made products), including the possibility to reset the product to its original state.

Evidence: hardening baseline as code (IaC module defaults); configuration tests in CI; documented factory-reset procedure.

Vulnerabilities addressable through security updates, including automatic updates installed within an appropriate timeframe, enabled by default, with a clear easy-to-use opt-out, update notifications, and the option to postpone temporarily.

Evidence: update mechanism and rollout pipeline; default-on auto-update UX with opt-out; published support-period end dates per product.

Protection from unauthorised access via appropriate control mechanisms (authentication, identity or access management), and reporting on possible unauthorised access.

Evidence: IdP-based authN/authZ; access-management design doc; alerts/reports on unauthorised-access events.

Confidentiality of stored, transmitted or processed data, personal or other, e.g. via state-of-the-art encryption at rest or in transit and other technical means.

Evidence: TLS configuration review; encryption-at-rest for persisted stores; key-management documentation.

Integrity of data, commands, programs and configuration against unauthorised manipulation, and reporting on corruptions.

Evidence: checksums/signatures on artifacts and updates; corruption detection and reporting in monitoring.

Data minimisation: only data adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary for the intended purpose.

Evidence: data-flow inventory; minimisation checkpoint in the design review template.

Availability of essential and basic functions, also after an incident, including resilience and mitigation measures against denial-of-service attacks.

Evidence: DoS resilience measures (rate limiting, degradation modes); incident recovery exercises with records.

Minimised negative impact on the availability of services provided by other devices or networks.

Evidence: backpressure/rate-limit design; network-hygiene test results.

Limited attack surfaces, including external interfaces, by design.

Evidence: interface inventory; exposed-surface review per release; removal records for dead interfaces.

Reduced incident impact via appropriate exploitation mitigation mechanisms and techniques.

Evidence: hardening flags (ASLR, sandboxing, memory-safe paths where feasible); penetration-test reports.

Security-related information by recording and monitoring relevant internal activity (access to or modification of data, services, functions), with a user opt-out.

Evidence: audit logging with retention and log-integrity protection; documented user opt-out.

Secure and easy permanent removal of all data and settings, and secure transfer to other products or systems where data can be transferred.

Evidence: deletion feature with verification test; secure export/transfer mechanism.

Annex I, Part II — vulnerability handling requirements

These are process duties on the manufacturer; Part II point 1 is a statutory SBOM duty.

Identify and document vulnerabilities and components, including a software bill of materials in a commonly used, machine-readable format covering at least top-level dependencies.

Evidence: SBOM generation in the release pipeline (CycloneDX or SPDX, e.g. via Syft); component inventory retained per release.

Address and remediate vulnerabilities without delay, including security updates; where technically feasible, security updates provided separately from functionality updates.

Evidence: patch SLA policy; separate security-update release path in the pipeline.

Effective and regular tests and reviews of the security of the product.

Evidence: recurring security test schedule (SAST/DAST/dependency scanning) with retained results.

Public disclosure of fixed vulnerabilities: description, affected-product identification, impacts, severity, and remediation guidance; delayed publication only in duly justified cases until users can patch.

Evidence: advisory publishing process (advisories page or OSV feed); disclosure log with dates.

A policy on coordinated vulnerability disclosure, put in place and enforced.

Evidence: CVD policy document, referenced from security.txt.

Facilitate sharing of vulnerability information for the product and third-party components, including a contact address for reports.

Evidence: security.txt on the product domain; intake process with triage owner.

Mechanisms to securely distribute updates, so vulnerabilities are fixed or mitigated in a timely manner, automatically where applicable.

Evidence: signed update artifacts; delivery infrastructure with integrity checks.

Security updates disseminated without delay and free of charge, accompanied by advisory messages with relevant information and potential actions.

Evidence: release-communication template including user-facing advisories.
