The launch we stopped 48 hours before go-live

A country entry at Wolt, a regulatory incompatibility six months of planning had missed, and the 23-market platform that made the next entries checkable.

Client
Wolt (food delivery, 23+ markets)
Engagement
Sep 2024 – Mar 2025
Role
Payments & compliance engineering
Stack
Kotlin, Kafka, Kubernetes, AWS, Terraform, Argo CD

What happened

North Macedonia was fully planned: six months of expansion work, launch date confirmed. Two days before go-live I found that the country’s 2013 accounting-standard change was incompatible with the ISO 4217 requirements our invoicing relied on — an “unknown unknown” that had slipped past senior leadership and every existing checklist.

I hard-blocked the launch. Blocking a country launch 48 hours before go-live is not a comfortable call; shipping invoices a tax authority cannot process is worse. I then led a 43-person crisis response together with Ernst & Young across engineering, Treasury and Merchant Payments.

The permanent fix

Post-mortems that end in slides end. This one ended in a platform: the centralized VAT management API serving all 23 European markets — event-driven Kotlin microservices processing 100K+ transactions a day across 23 VAT regimes, replacing hardcoded implementations scattered across 50+ microservices. Market-specific rules became data with owners and tests instead of folklore in fifty codebases.

The compliance framework that came out of the crisis is now mandatory for every new market entry. On top of it, I delivered Peppol-based e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) for Greece and prepared the Hungarian and Bulgarian rollouts, and handed Treasury, Merchant Payments and engineering comprehensive documentation so the machine ran without me.

What it proves

Regulatory risk does not announce itself. It sits in the gap between what a business assumes and what a rule actually says — and it surfaces at the worst possible moment unless someone systematically goes looking for it early, with the primary sources open.

Why this is CRA work. The Cyber Resilience Act is the same shape of problem at product scope: a regulation with hard dates, categories and evidence duties, whose expensive surprises live exactly in the “we assumed” zone. My readiness assessments exist to find the North-Macedonia-grade blockers months early: classification first, Annex I gap analysis second, evidence built into the pipeline — not discovered in the launch week.
