Where you stand

The clock is already running

The 24-hour clock applies today

Since 11 September 2026, an actively exploited vulnerability or severe incident in a product on the EU market starts a 24 h early-warning clock, then a 72 h main notification — through ENISA’s Single Reporting Platform, and for products shipped before the application date too.

“We have until December 2027” is the expensive misunderstanding

Full application lands 11 December 2027, but classification, documentation and reporting duties bite earlier. The harmonised standards (request M/606) are already late — extended by three months on 10 November 2026 — so early movers self-assess against Annex I directly instead of waiting.

The stake is EU market access

Up to €15 million or 2.5% of worldwide turnover — and withdrawal of CE marking: products can be pulled from the Union market. If you are US-listed, SOX rides on top; about two-thirds of the control work is one shared fabric.

How I work

Four rules I do not bend

  1. Primary sources, not marketing

    The Official Journal text, the U.S. Code, ENISA’s own platform — read on the day, cited so you can check. When something is analyst knowledge rather than a verified page, it is labelled as such.

  2. Evidence born machine-readable

    Compliance that rests on screenshots and chat approvals is a deficiency factory. Controls should emit their own audit evidence as a byproduct of shipping: MR approvals, CI records, IdP logs, SBOMs — retained immutably.

  3. Build the fabric once, map it twice

    The CRA and SOX share roughly two-thirds of their underlying controls. One investment, two reporting formats — and separate attestations, because an ICFR opinion says nothing about CE marking.

  4. Verified and inferred, always labelled

    Every claim I hand you carries its provenance — the same discipline my research reports hold themselves to. If I could not verify it, you will know before a regulator does.
