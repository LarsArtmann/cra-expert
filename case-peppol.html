At Wolt: delivering into the network

Part of the payments platform work at Wolt was Peppol-based e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) for Greece — delivered into production — with the Hungarian and Bulgarian rollouts prepared on the same rails. E-invoicing in these markets is not an integration project; it is a compliance obligation with formats, deadlines and a certified network in the middle. It sits directly on top of the VAT management platform described in the market-entry case.

At primeXchange: inside the Access Point

Later I worked as Freelance Software Architect & Platform Engineer at primeXchange, a certified Peppol Access Point and SMP (Service Metadata Publisher) serving Germany, France, Greece, Spain, Poland and further markets with XRechnung, ZUGFeRD and classic EDI. My side: optimising the document data pipelines, leading IT crisis management, running the Linux/Kubernetes/Longhorn platform, and introducing distributed OpenTelemetry metrics with Python automation.

From inside the four-corner model you learn what regulated infrastructure actually demands: every document provably processed, participants validated, formats enforced to the schema, errors surfaced to humans in time — and the whole thing operable by a small team.

What it proves

Standards-bound, deadline-bound, certification-bound regimes are where I have spent the last years — not as an observer but as the engineer accountable for the pipes. E-invoicing was the rehearsal; product cybersecurity under the CRA is the same discipline at wider scope.

Why this is CRA work. The CRA inherits the exact structure of these regimes — harmonised standards, conformity routes, certified third parties (notified bodies instead of Access Points), and statutory clocks — and applies it to products with digital elements.
